The structure of the courts

India has a single, integrated judiciary. Unlike federal systems with separate state and national courts, every court sits in one hierarchy with the Supreme Court at the top, and its rulings bind all courts below (Article 141).

Supreme Court of India

New Delhi · Articles 124–147 · Chief Justice of India plus up to 33 judges · judges retire at 65

High Courts

25 High Courts for the states and union territories · Articles 214–231 · judges retire at 62

District & Sessions Courts

District Judge (civil) and Sessions Judge (criminal) in each district

Subordinate Courts

Civil Judges (Senior & Junior Division), Judicial Magistrates, Metropolitan Magistrates

Supreme Court

  • Original jurisdiction in disputes between the Union and states (Art. 131).
  • Writ jurisdiction to enforce fundamental rights (Art. 32), which Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called "the very soul of the Constitution".
  • Appellate jurisdiction over High Courts, including Special Leave Petitions (Art. 136).
  • Advisory jurisdiction when the President refers a question (Art. 143).

High Courts

  • Highest court in a state, and some cover several states or UTs.
  • Writ powers under Art. 226, wider than the Supreme Court's: they cover legal rights as well as fundamental ones.
  • Supervise all subordinate courts in their territory (Art. 227).
  • The Calcutta High Court (1862) is the oldest.

District Courts

  • Where most citizens meet the justice system.
  • Hear civil suits, criminal trials, family, property and succession matters.
  • A Sessions Court tries serious offences and can pass any sentence the law allows. A death sentence needs High Court confirmation.

Beyond the hierarchy

  • Tribunals: NCLT (companies), NGT (environment), ITAT (tax), CAT (government service), and others.
  • Consumer Commissions at district, state and national level.
  • Family Courts and Gram Nyayalayas (village courts).
  • Lok Adalats: "people's courts" that settle disputes by compromise. Their awards are final, with no appeal.

Key doctrines to know

Basic Structure
Kesavananda Bharati (1973): Parliament can amend the Constitution but cannot destroy its basic structure.
Collegium
Senior Supreme Court judges recommend appointments and transfers of higher judges, a system that grew out of the "Judges Cases" (1981, 1993, 1998).
Public Interest Litigation
Since the 1980s, any public-spirited person can approach the courts on behalf of those who cannot, sometimes with just a letter.
Separation of powers
Art. 50 directs the state to separate the judiciary from the executive.

Who is a वकील?

Vakeel (from the Arabic wakīl, "one who is entrusted") is the everyday word in Hindi and Urdu for a lawyer. In law, the formal title is Advocate, governed by the Advocates Act, 1961 and regulated by the Bar Council of India and the State Bar Councils.

Advocate

Enrolled with a State Bar Council and entitled to practise in any court or tribunal in India (Section 30, Advocates Act).

Senior Advocate

Designated by the Supreme Court or a High Court for ability and standing at the Bar (Section 16). Senior Advocates wear distinct gowns and usually appear with a junior or an Advocate-on-Record.

Advocate-on-Record

Only an AoR can file cases in the Supreme Court. This requires years of practice, training under a senior AoR, and passing a special Supreme Court exam.

Public Prosecutor

Represents the State in criminal cases. Prosecutors and the Attorney General / Advocate General are the government's lawyers.

What a vakeel does

  • Advises clients on their rights and options
  • Drafts plaints, petitions, bail applications, contracts and notices
  • Argues cases and cross-examines witnesses
  • Negotiates settlements and represents clients in mediation
  • Protects confidential communications with clients (BSA, 2023)

The dress code

Black coat or robe, white shirt and white neck bands. These symbolise the advocate's duty and the purity of justice. Gowns are required in the Supreme Court and High Courts.

Professional ethics

The Bar Council rules forbid advocates from advertising or soliciting work. An advocate must not refuse a brief without good reason, and owes duties to the court, the client, the opponent and colleagues.

How to become a vakeel

  1. 1

    Get a law degree

    A 5-year integrated course (BA/BBA LLB etc.) after Class 12, often through CLAT for National Law Universities, or a 3-year LLB after graduation.

  2. 2

    Enrol with a State Bar Council

    Apply with your degree and documents to the Bar Council of the state where you'll practise.

  3. 3

    Pass the AIBE

    The All India Bar Examination, run by the Bar Council of India, gives you the Certificate of Practice.

  4. 4

    Practise and specialise

    Most advocates start as juniors in a senior's chamber, then build a practice in criminal, civil, corporate, tax, family or constitutional law.

Want to be a judge instead? Fresh law graduates can take the state Judicial Services exams to become Civil Judges. Some posts now require prior years at the Bar.

Free legal aid: justice is a right

Article 39A of the Constitution directs the State to provide free legal aid, and the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 makes it work through NALSA, State and District Legal Services Authorities.

Who is eligible

  • Women and children
  • Members of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes
  • Persons with disabilities
  • Victims of trafficking, disasters or ethnic violence
  • Industrial workmen and people in custody
  • People below the income limit set by their state

Rights every citizen should know

  • An arrested person must be told the grounds of arrest and may consult a lawyer of their choice (Art. 22).
  • They must be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours.
  • No one can be forced to be a witness against themselves (Art. 20(3)).
  • A Zero FIR can be filed at any police station, whatever the jurisdiction.

Courtroom glossary

Vakalatnama
The document a client signs to authorise an advocate to act for them.
Adalat
Court.
Nyayadhish
Judge.
Muvakkil
Client.
Tareekh
Date of hearing. "Tareekh pe tareekh" is the famous complaint about adjournments.
FIR
First Information Report, the police's first record of a cognisable offence.
Bail / Zamanat
Release from custody pending trial, on conditions.
Plaint
The written complaint that starts a civil suit.
Habeas Corpus
A writ ordering that a detained person be produced in court.
Mandamus
A writ ordering a public authority to do its duty.
SLP
Special Leave Petition, a request to the Supreme Court to hear an appeal.
Affidavit / Halafnama
A written statement sworn on oath.
